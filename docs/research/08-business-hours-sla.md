# Business Hours, Timezones & SLA Policies in Helpdesk Software — Research Report (Aug 2026)

## 1. Zendesk's model

**Schedules.** Admin Center > Schedules: each schedule has a single timezone, weekly business-hours intervals, and a per-schedule holiday list (holidays can be scheduled up to 2 years ahead; SLA clocks pause during them). Suite Team/Growth/Professional (and Support Professional) get **one schedule per account**; **multiple schedules are Enterprise-only**. With multiple schedules, the schedule is applied *per ticket via a trigger* (e.g., "Brand = AU → Set schedule: Sydney"), and SLAs/views/automations then use the ticket's schedule. This trigger-plumbing is a recurring setup complaint — nothing applies a schedule by requester location out of the box.

**SLA policies** (Suite Growth+ / Support Professional+). A policy = conditions (group, org, brand, tags, channel, priority…) + targets per priority. Seven metrics: **First reply time, Next reply time, Periodic update, Pausable update, Requester wait time, Agent work time, Total resolution time**. Pause semantics are status-driven: Requester wait time pauses on Pending; Agent work time pauses on Pending + On-hold; Pausable update runs only in New/Open/On-hold; First reply, Next reply, Periodic update, and Total resolution never pause. Each target, per priority, is independently measured in **business hours or calendar hours** — you can give Urgent a calendar-hours target and Low a business-hours one. Policies are ordered; first match wins. **Group SLAs (OLAs)** — target "ownership time" per internal group, with an Explore dashboard — are gated to **Suite Enterprise ($169/agent/mo)**.

**Breach handling.** No native breach notification. The standard recipe: automations using **"Hours until next SLA breach"** (business-hours aware) + a guard tag like `sla_warning_sent`, otherwise the hourly automation spams ~8 duplicate alerts per ticket. Agent UX: an **SLA badge with live countdown** in views; hover reveals all active targets and exact breach datetime; sort the SLA column ascending to get a "next breach first" queue. Known gaps users cite: the badge disappears once a target is achieved/breached (breached tickets vanish from the countdown sort), the "Next SLA breach" filter excludes already-breached tickets, and views can't show Requester wait time in the SLA column (open community idea). Another long-running community thread demands a "Pausable Periodic Update" metric. DST: schedules auto-adjust at changeover, but ticket timestamps are stored UTC and rendered in the viewer's *current* offset, so pre-changeover events display an hour off — a documented confusion source.

## 2. Freshdesk's model

**Business hours** (Growth+): each Business Hours object = timezone + working hours (uniform or per-day) + holiday calendar with **importable country holiday packs**. **Multiple business hours are Pro/Enterprise** (legacy Estate/Forest) and are attached **per group** — an "EU" group and an "APAC" group each get their own clock, and a ticket's SLA runs on its assigned group's hours.

**SLA policies**: one policy on Growth; **multiple policies on Pro/Enterprise** (up to 1,000/account, ordered first-match). Conditions: Company, Group, Product, Source, Type, contact/company **segments**, Contact Type, and (Enterprise) custom fields with up to 5 condition levels — this is how per-account SLAs ("Acme gets 15-min response") are built. Targets per priority: **First response, Every response** (their next-reply equivalent), **Resolution**, each switchable business/calendar hours. **Reminders**: separate pre-breach reminders for first response, next response, and resolution, configurable only **5 min–4 h before due** (a community feature request asks for per-priority reminder times). **Escalation**: email/in-app; only **one escalation rule for response breaches** but up to **4 escalation levels for resolution breaches** — an asymmetry MSP-type users dislike; teams bolt on Zenduty/PagerDuty for real paging. Agent UX: a **"Next response due"** timestamp per ticket, filterable/sortable ticket views by next-response due, and agents can **manually extend an SLA deadline** after agreeing with the customer — a genuinely liked feature Zendesk lacks.

## 3. Multi-region reality (e.g., Australia)

The canonical Zendesk pattern is its **"Configuring a follow-the-sun global support model"** guide: Enterprise multiple schedules + triggers that reassign the ticket group based on which regional schedule is "within business hours," so an AU ticket at 9am AEST routes to the Sydney group. Priority SLAs ("Enterprise orgs: 15-min first reply during Sydney hours") are done via org- or segment-conditioned policies with business-hours targets against the AU schedule. **Showing the customer's local clock is not native anywhere major** — Zendesk relies on marketplace apps (**World Clock**, which reads the contact's Time zone field and shows a day/night icon; **Time Zone Converter** in the ticket sidebar). Intercom shows user local time on profiles, but no mainstream desk renders "it's 3:42pm Tuesday in Sydney" inline for agents. This is a cheap, high-value differentiator: infer timezone from phone/IP/address, display it on every ticket, and warn agents composing replies at the customer's 2am.

## 4. Complaints and what MSP tools do better

Themes from G2/Capterra/communities: Zendesk — plan-gating resentment (multiple schedules and Group SLAs behind Enterprise), configuration requiring trigger/automation gymnastics for basics like breach alerts, steep learning curve. Freshdesk — weak escalation depth and reminder caps, reporting limitations. MSP forums' core demand: *"the ticket should know which client, which contract, and which SLA clock applies without a tech choosing from a dropdown."*

**HaloPSA**: SLAs reference **Workdays** (Config > SLAs > Workdays) with per-day hours and a **timezone override per workday**; holidays attach to each workday (users complain there's no global holiday list — active ideas-portal request); SLAs bind to customers/contracts so client-specific terms auto-apply; dashboards surface "impending SLA violations" for wallboards. **ConnectWise PSA**: SLAs live on **Agreements** (per-client contracts) with custom calendars; tracks three clocks — **Respond by, Resolution plan by, Resolved by** — plus an **Impact × Urgency priority matrix**, and five escalation states including "We are waiting (do not escalate)." The takeaway: MSP engines treat SLA as a *contract attribute of the customer*, not a workflow rule — helpdesks make you rebuild that with conditions.

## 5. Design details worth copying

- **Status-driven pausing** with explicit, documented semantics per metric; offer a "pausable periodic update" (Zendesk's missing metric).
- **Per-target business/calendar choice per priority**; measure against the *ticket's* schedule, but let a policy pin a schedule directly (skip trigger plumbing).
- **Countdown badges in ticket lists + "sort by next breach"** — and keep breached tickets visible/sortable (fixing Zendesk's badge-disappears flaw).
- **Native pre-breach reminders and multi-level escalation for every metric**, business-hours aware, no tag hacks.
- **Per-account SLA via customer/segment/contract**, with per-plan-tier defaults (Enterprise 15 min, Free 24 h) as first-class objects.
- **Holiday calendars as shared, importable country packs** reusable across schedules (Freshdesk's import + fixing Halo's per-workday duplication).
- **DST**: store UTC + IANA zone, auto-shift schedules at changeover, and render historical timestamps in the offset in effect *at event time*.
- **Customer local time on every ticket** with day/night indicator and "outside their hours" compose warning.
- **SLA attainment reporting** out of the box (Zendesk needs Explore dashboards; Halo ships SLA dashboards natively) — % achieved by policy/metric/priority, breach root-cause drilldown.
- Agent-facing **"next response due"** field + manual SLA extension with audit trail (Freshdesk).
