# Market Survey: The Rest of the Field (August 2026)

## Vendor-by-Vendor

**Intercom** positions itself as the "AI-first customer service platform," anchored by its Fin AI agent (2M+ AI-resolved conversations/week). Pricing is per-seat — $29 (Essential), $85 (Advanced), $132 (Expert) per seat/month annual — plus Fin at $0.99 per resolution stacked on every plan. Standout feature: Fin, widely regarded as the best-performing AI support agent, now also sold standalone on top of Salesforce, HubSpot, Freshworks, Zoho, Front, and Gorgias helpdesks. In June 2026 Salesforce signed a definitive agreement to acquire Fin for ~$3.6B (unclosed as of July 2026) — a major uncertainty overhang for Intercom customers. Biggest weakness: cost unpredictability and trust. G2 rating is 4.5/5 (~3,855 reviews) but Trustpilot sits at 1.9/5 (950+ reviews); documented cases include "$200/month → $1,400 during a product launch because of AI resolution volume," plus recurring complaints of slow billing support and outages during which "customer submissions are lost entirely."

**Help Scout** is the "simple, human" player — a shared-inbox helpdesk for SMBs that markets ease of use against Zendesk. Current pricing: Free (5 users, 1 inbox, 1 Docs site), Standard $25/user/mo, Plus $45, Pro $75 (annual; $30/$54/$90 monthly), plus AI Answers at $0.75/resolution. Standout: genuinely fast setup and a well-loved shared inbox + Beacon widget; ~4.4/5 on G2 and beats Zendesk on ease-of-use/setup scores. Biggest weakness: self-inflicted trust damage — in late 2024 it switched from per-seat to per-contacts-helped pricing, triggered churn and a loud backlash ("Helpscout lost all trust with this flip-flopping on pricing"), and by 2026 quietly reverted to per-user pricing (legacy customers remain on contact-based billing).

**Zoho Desk** is the value/feature-density play. Annual pricing: Express $7, Standard $14, Professional $23, Enterprise $40/agent/mo (monthly billing is 52–60% higher: $9/$20/$35/$50); free plan for 3 agents. Enterprise at $40 is roughly a third of Zendesk Suite Professional ($115). Standout feature: **Blueprint** (Professional+) — a visual state-machine builder for ticket lifecycles — plus strong workflow automation. Biggest weakness: the most common complaint is Enterprise-only gating of AI (Zia); also a steep learning curve, a "little slow" interface, weak mobile apps, and live chat delivered via a separate product (Zoho SalesIQ) and only at Enterprise. The Zoho-suite UI feels dated versus modern rivals.

**Front** is "customer operations" — a collaborative shared inbox for teams that live in email (logistics, agencies, B2B services), not a classic ticketing portal. In 2026 it simplified from four plans to three: Starter $25, Professional $65, Enterprise $105/seat/mo annual; 14-day trial, no free plan, two-seat minimum. Standout: the multichannel shared inbox with internal comments/@mentions on real email threads. Biggest weaknesses: expensive for SMBs; AI (Copilot, Autopilot, QA, CSAT) sold as paid add-ons stacking on premium seats; annual contracts with a strict no-refund policy; unreliable key integrations (HubSpot's is poorly rated, forcing manual data entry). No real end-customer knowledge-base/portal depth.

**HubSpot Service Hub** sells "support inside your CRM." Tiers: Free, Starter (~$15/seat/mo annual, $20 monthly), Professional ~$90–100/seat, Enterprise $150/seat with a 10-seat minimum (real floor $1,500/mo). Hidden costs: mandatory onboarding fees ($1,500 Pro, $3,500 Enterprise), and as of April 14, 2026 its **Customer Agent** AI bills $0.50 (50 HubSpot Credits) per resolved conversation, Pro/Enterprise only. Standout: native unified CRM context — tickets, deals, and marketing data in one record. Biggest weaknesses: the Starter→Professional cliff (Core Seat $20 → $50, and "Service Seats" with basic collaboration features only exist on Pro/Enterprise), and email automation workflows requiring a separate Marketing Hub purchase — classic suite-upsell mechanics that G2 reviewers cite as the top con.

**Crisp** is the budget all-in-one (chat-first) for small businesses, and notably the only flat-rate player: Free plan, Mini $45/workspace/mo (4 seats), Essentials $95 (10 seats), Plus $295 (20 seats), extra agents ~€10/seat. Standout: predictable per-workspace pricing plus a wide bundle (chat, shared inbox, basic CRM, KB, campaigns, chatbot). Biggest weaknesses: unlimited AI requires jumping $95 → $295 (a 210% increase for one feature); limited reporting on all plans; a CRM reviewers call basic; delayed/unreliable mobile and email notifications; complaints about add-on pricing and cancellation/billing flows.

## Underserved Segments

1. **Teams needing internal SOPs + external KB + helpdesk in one product.** No surveyed vendor does this. Support teams currently bolt on Trainual ($199/mo Train, $299/mo Scale, ~$1,000 implementation fee), Tettra (Slack-based, "Kai" AI assistant), Notion, or Scribe/Tango for SOPs, separate from their helpdesk's customer-facing KB. A first-class SOP section with role-based assignment inside the support tool is a genuine white space.
2. **SMBs priced out of AI.** Every incumbent gates AI behind expensive tiers (Zoho: Enterprise-only; Crisp: $295 plan; HubSpot: Pro+ plus credits; Front: add-ons) or bills unpredictable per-resolution fees (Intercom $0.99, Help Scout $0.75, HubSpot $0.50). Predictable, included AI at SMB prices is unserved.
3. **Flat/predictable pricing seekers.** Only Crisp offers flat workspace pricing, and it's weak on ticketing/reporting. Per-seat + usage-based AI is the universal complaint theme across G2/Reddit.
4. **Multi-tier user models.** Customer portals are gated to upper tiers (Help Scout's "secure customer portal" only at Plus $45; light users only at Pro). Multi-tenant/multi-brand KB portals (agencies, multi-product companies) are essentially absent — Help Scout caps Docs sites per plan (2 on Plus, 10 on Pro), forcing upgrades unrelated to seat count.

## Help Scout Deep Dive

**Gets right:** fastest setup in the category; clean shared inbox with collision detection; broad channel coverage (email, live chat, SMS, WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger) on all plans; a real free plan (5 users); customer-friendly brand; high ease-of-use ratings that outscore Zendesk. **Falls short:** Docs KB has **zero version control** (no history, no rollback), no auto-translation, no multi-tenant portal delivery; Docs-site caps by plan; AI Answers is web-chat only, read-only (answers from Docs but can't take actions like look up an order or process a refund), and costs $0.75/resolution; proactive chat, advanced routing, and chatbot customization are limited or missing; no internal SOP/training concept at all; and the 2024–25 pricing flip-flop burned trust that a competitor can explicitly target ("pricing that never changes underneath you").

## Fin AI Pricing and Sentiment

Fin costs **$0.99 per "outcome"** on all plans with a 50-outcome/month minimum (~$49). Billable outcomes: a resolution, a procedure handoff, or a disqualification at $0.99; lead qualification is $9.99. A resolution refund exists (if a customer returns to a "resolved" conversation, the charge is reversed, even across billing periods). Customer sentiment on per-resolution pricing is sharply negative on predictability and definition-gaming: Reddit reports include a ~40-agent team going "$4k/month → $9k" ("way more complicated than their marketing page suggests"), a legacy customer going $119 → $854/month after migrating, and a projection of $1,200 → $10,000/month. Intercom's own community forum thread "Fin's flawed assumed resolved & pricing design" documents Fin marking conversations "assumed resolved" even when a human stepped in to fix Fin's wrong answer — i.e., billing $0.99 for non-resolutions. The structural paradox customers name: the better the AI performs, the higher the bill, making success unbudgetable. This is the single most actionable positioning wedge for a new entrant: flat-rate or included AI.
