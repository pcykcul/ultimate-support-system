# Human-Only Support Positioning: Evidence Brief (Aug 2026)

## 1. Consumer sentiment: the demand is real and measurable

- **Gartner (Dec 2023 survey, n=5,728; published July 2024):** [64% of customers would prefer that companies didn't use AI for customer service](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service), and **53% would consider switching to a competitor** if a company used AI in service. The #1 stated fear: AI will make it harder to reach a human.
- **SurveyMonkey (Dec 2025, n=2,017 US adults):** [79% strongly prefer speaking with a human over an AI agent](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/customer-service-statistics/); 61% say humans understand needs better, 84% believe humans are more accurate; only 8% prefer AI.
- **Medallia, 2026 State of CX (n=1,500+, US/UK/CA/AU/NZ):** [75%+ say dealing exclusively with humans "always" or "often" produces better outcomes; 80%+ report greater loyalty to companies that prioritize human service; 67% believe human service is becoming a premium/luxury tier](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/human-driven-customer-service-luxury-premium/816501/).
- **Willingness to pay:** [42% of consumers would pay extra for access to human reps (Kinsta), and 93% prefer a human agent overall](https://www.answerconnect.com/blog/business-tips/ai-human-customer-service-statistics/); a [YouGov/Zurich survey](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/human-driven-customer-service-luxury-premium/816501/) found consumers will pay more for human, empathetic service.
- **Trust:** Salesforce's [State of the Connected Customer](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ai-customer-research/) shows consumer openness to AI **fell from 65% (2022) to 51% (2024)**; only 57% trust companies to use AI ethically; 68% say AI makes trustworthiness more important.
- **Chatbot failure behavior:** [30% of consumers abandon a brand after one bad chatbot experience](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gilpress/2023/02/01/one-negative-chatbot-experience-drives-away-30-of-customers/) (UJET); roundups report [82% want a human option, 54% sometimes find no path to a live agent, and 71% feel deceived when a bot pretends to be human](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/customer-service-statistics.html).

**Takeaway:** the positioning is not contrarian — it aligns with majority preference, and the trust delta is widening as AI adoption grows.

## 2. Companies already selling human support as the product

- **T-Mobile "Team of Experts"** ([launched Aug 2018](https://www.t-mobile.com/news/press/introducing-tex): "Real People, Not Robots. No bots, no bouncing, no BS"): every postpaid customer is assigned a dedicated 30–40-person team in their region; no automated phone menus. Results: [NPS up 60%, postpaid churn down 39%, and the **lowest cost-to-serve in company history**](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20181018005225/en/) — proof human-first can be an economics story, not just a CX story.
- **Zappos:** 24/7 phone number (800-927-7671) promoted [at the top of the site](https://www.zappos.com/c/contact-us); no call scripts or time limits; the famous [10-hour-51-minute service call](https://hyken.com/customer-service/customer-service-legend/) was celebrated, not punished.
- **Chewy:** operationalized empathy at scale — [1,000+ commissioned pet portraits shipped weekly](https://www.customercontactweekdigital.com/customer-experience/articles/chewys-unique-customer-gifts-win-over-loyal-pet-owners), handwritten signed cards, flowers on pet loss, refunds converted to shelter donations.
- **Basecamp/37signals:** all-email human support with a [~20-minute typical first-response time](https://basecamp.com/support); a [testimonials page naming individual agents](https://basecamp.com/support/testimonials); senior "on-call" programmers who fix root causes surfaced by support ([REWORK podcast](https://37signals.com/podcast/support-console-ation/)).
- **Discover:** long-running "[talk to a real human any time, 24/7](https://www.discover.com/credit-cards/contact-us/)" / 100% U.S.-based service positioning in national TV ads.
- **Lush:** the anti-tech archetype — [quit Instagram/Facebook/TikTok/Snapchat in Nov 2021](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/lush-quitting-social-media-with-new-anti-social-media-policy/2021112359525); CEO Mark Constantine said he was ["happy to lose £10m"](https://globalnews.ca/news/8392430/lush-cosmetics-deactivating-social-media) over it; [still largely off social in 2025](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/02/24/lush-anti-social-media-strategy).
- **Smaller proof points:** UK host [Krystal](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/krystal.io) markets "speak to a real human without waiting"; **cautionary tale:** Nationwide Building Society's human/branch-promise ads with Dominic West drew 281 complaints and an [ASA ban for overclaiming](https://www.marketingweek.com/nationwide-ads-banned-closures/) — never promise more humanity than you deliver.

## 3. The anti-AI / "AI-free" trend and public AI-support failures

**The label trend (2025–26):** "100% human" is being called [the new "organic"](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/anti-ai-marketing/). Documented cases ([DesignRush roundup](https://news.designrush.com/no-ai-disclaimers-brands-consumer-trust-2026)): **Aerie** pledged never to use AI-generated people (Q4 2025 sales +23% YoY); **Dove** formally pledged never to use AI to depict women; **Cadbury**, **Polaroid** ("Real stories. Not stories and reels"), **LEGO** (#HonestlyItsNotAI), **Almond Breeze** ("No AI Needed" with the Jonas Brothers). **iHeartMedia** made ["Guaranteed Human" a core on-air brand across all stations in 2026](https://www.insideradio.com/free/iheartmedia-makes-guaranteed-human-a-core-branding-message-across-all-stations/article_3ad0b04f-76ba-4466-8839-5a4bdce798a1.html), citing internal research that [90% of consumers want media from a real human](https://www.iheartmedia.com/advertise/insights/articles/guaranteed-human) — but faced ["gimmick" criticism after June 2026 layoffs](https://barrettmedia.com/2026/06/25/iheartmedia-reconsider-guaranteed-human-layoffs/). Gartner (2025, n=1,539): 68% frequently wonder whether online content is genuine. Verdict: **"AI-free" resonates when operationally true; it backfires fast when it's just copy.**

**Named failures to cite in sales decks:**
- **Klarna:** AI assistant (Feb 2024) did "[the work of 700 agents](https://getperspective.ai/blog/klarna-ai-customer-service-replacing-700-agents-conversational-ai-case-study)," 2.3M chats in month one. By **May 2025** CEO Siemiatkowski reversed: ["We focused too much on efficiency and cost... lower quality"](https://www.forbes.com/sites/quickerbettertech/2025/05/18/business-tech-news-klarna-reverses-on-ai-says-customers-like-talking-to-people/) — Klarna [resumed hiring human agents](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396) and now guarantees customers can always reach a human.
- **Air Canada (Moffatt v. Air Canada, Feb 2024):** chatbot invented a retroactive bereavement-fare policy; BC tribunal [rejected Air Canada's argument that the chatbot was "a separate legal entity"](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/moffatt-v-air-canada-misrepresentation-ai-chatbot) and awarded [CAD $812.02](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/air-canada-chatbot-lawsuit-1.7116416). Precedent: you are liable for what your bot says.
- **Cursor (April 2025):** support bot "Sam" [hallucinated a nonexistent single-device login policy](https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/18/cursor_ai_support_bot_lies/); users publicly [canceled subscriptions](https://fortune.com/article/customer-support-ai-cursor-went-rogue) before the cofounder apologized. The kicker: the bot used a human name with no AI label — exactly the deception 71% of consumers resent.

## 4. Economics of human-only support

- **Cost gap is real:** human-assisted tickets run a [~$13.50 median (Gartner), $2.70–12 retail, $25–35 SaaS](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/customer-service-cost-per-ticket), vs. [$0.10–1.50 per AI-resolved ticket claimed by vendors](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ai-agent-vs-human-agent-cost) (~$0.50 vs ~$6 in Freshworks/Salesforce benchmarks). You can't out-cheap AI per ticket — you win on **retention economics** (T-Mobile: churn −39% at lowest-ever cost-to-serve) and price the humanity in (42% will pay extra).
- **Deflect without bots:** a plain, well-maintained knowledge base deflects a [median 18% of tickets (range 5–35%); teams with 50–200 regularly updated articles hit 20–35%](https://alhena.ai/blog/what-is-deflection-rate/) — no AI required. Add macros/snippets for the repetitive 40–60% of volume, keeping a human deciding and signing every send.
- **Keeping response times low with small teams:** Basecamp holds ~20-minute email turnaround by making email the primary channel (async load-leveling), staffing to published business hours, and rotating specialists; larger orgs use follow-the-sun. Klarna's post-reversal ["Uber-style" remote agent pool](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/klarna-hires-customer-service-after-ai-pivot) (students, parents, rural workers on flexible schedules) is a viable elastic-staffing model for peaks.

## 5. Execution details worth copying

1. **Named humans, visible:** agent's real first name + photo + signature in every reply (Basecamp names reps publicly; Chewy's cards are hand-signed; T-Mobile assigns a persistent named team per city).
2. **Time-bound human promise:** "A real person — [name] — will reply by [time]" beats vague "we care." Publish the measured first-response median (Basecamp's 20 minutes is itself marketing).
3. **Honest automation only:** auto-receipts allowed but explicitly labeled ("This is an automated receipt — the human reply comes next from Maria"). Never a bot with a human name (the Cursor "Sam" lesson; 71% feel deceived).
4. **No phone tree / no chat-widget maze:** phone number or email at the top of every page (Zappos), zero IVR menus (T-Mobile's "no robots, no bouncing").
5. **Empower, don't script:** no scripts, no handle-time quotas, discretionary budget for gestures (Chewy portraits, Zappos' 10h51m call).
6. **Substantiate the claim:** put the "no AI ever reads or writes your ticket" pledge in the ToS/marketing only if operations match — the Nationwide ASA ban and iHeart layoff backlash show overclaiming converts the differentiator into a liability.

**Bottom line:** 2024–2026 data shows majority preference (64–79%), switching intent (53%), loyalty premiums (80%+), and payment willingness (42%) for human service, while Klarna, Air Canada, and Cursor supply named proof that AI support carries quality, legal, and churn risk. "No AI" positioning works when it is an operational fact — named agents, published human response times, honestly-labeled automation, and a strong self-service KB that keeps the human team affordable.
